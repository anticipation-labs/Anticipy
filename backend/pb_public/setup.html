Three honest things first

  • This is a developer install. Chrome calls it Load unpacked — you download a folder and you keep it, and Chrome reads it from wherever it sits. Move it or delete it and I stop working.
  • It won't update itself. When there's a new version I'll tell you, and you'll download it again and press the refresh arrow on Chrome's extensions page. The Chrome Web Store version, when it's ready, will update on its own.
  • You'll see a yellow bar. While I'm working, Chrome puts a strip across the top of your tabs saying “Anticipy started debugging this browser.” That's Chrome being honest that something other than you is clicking. It goes when the task is done.

  1. Step 1 Download the folder

    Download Anticipy for Chrome

    anticipy-claude-version-extension.zip — always the current build. It comes from Anticipy's own server, the same one your phone talks to. Once it is running, Anticipy knows which version you have.

    The download button is right here when you open this page on your computer.

    Then unzip it. This bit differs by machine:

    • Mac — double-click the file. You get a folder called anticipy-claude-version-extension next to it.
    • Windows — right-click the file, choose Extract All, then Extract. Don't just double-click it: that opens a read-only peek inside the zip, and Chrome can't load from there.

    Put the folder somewhere you won't tidy away later — your Documents, not your Downloads.

  2. Step 2 Open Chrome's extensions page

    Chrome won't let a web page link to its own settings, so this one is copy and paste. Put this in Chrome's address bar and press Enter.

    chrome://extensions

  3. Step 3 Turn on Developer mode

    Top-right corner of that page, a switch marked Developer mode. It's the only way Chrome will accept a folder instead of a store listing. You can leave it on.

  4. Step 4 Load the folder

    Three buttons appear at the top-left. Click Load unpacked and choose the anticipy-claude-version-extension folder itself — select the folder, don't open it and pick a file inside.

  5. Step 5 Pair it with your phone

    A welcome tab opens on its own with a 6-digit code. If you closed it, click the Anticipy icon in Chrome's toolbar — you may have to click the puzzle-piece icon first — and the code is in there too.

    The code goes into the app, in Settings. It does not go into setting the app up: that is four screens — her introduction, then How it works, May I listen? and Where should I reach you?, counting themselves 2 of 4, 3 of 4, 4 of 4 along the top — and not one of them asks for a code. If a screen is asking for your phone number, you have not done anything wrong; you are on the last one.

    So finish it first if you are still in there: Start living your day on that last screen, or Skip for now. Either lands you on the home screen. From there:

    • Tap the three sliders at the top right of the home screen to open Settings.
    • Scroll down to Your computer. It sits below Listening, Pendant, You, Your voice and Your number, so it is a good way down the page.
    • Type the code into the box marked 6-digit code from the extension. There is no button to press — it sends itself the moment the sixth digit lands. The Status line goes from Not paired to Paired, and once Chrome checks in it reads Live · seen 3s ago.

    Back on the home screen, the Chrome chip reads Chrome ready. That's everything — you can close the extensions page.

What I will and won't do in there

You say yes once, in the app or by text, before I start. Then I carry out that exact thing in Chrome without asking again for every step of it — but if what I find doesn't match what you agreed to, I stop and come back to you: a different price, a different date, a fee nobody mentioned.

I never type a password or a card number. Banking, brokerage and payment sites are on a blocklist that stops me in code — not something I decide in the moment. A login screen, or a check asking you to prove you're human, ends the task and hands it back to you.
