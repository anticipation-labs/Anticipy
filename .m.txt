A scorer, and the honest finding that it cannot yet score anything

I built ground truth by cutting his 244 logged lines wherever he went quiet
for more than ten minutes, then scored links against it. Links lost badly:
19 conversations against the timer's 6, on a truth of 3.

The result is meaningless, and so is its opposite. The ground truth is itself
a TIMER - a 600s one - so a link method was being marked against the very
mechanism it exists to replace. Circular by construction. The sister branch
flagged this same trap in its own report and I walked into it anyway.

Then I read what the truth labels actually contain. The largest "one
conversation" is 37 lines running: greetings, a direct question to Claude
about speaker recognition, his local-first architecture ruling, and a Wispr
dictation of a numbered list. At least four unrelated things, called one
conversation solely because he typed them inside ten minutes.

The model answering "0, this starts something new" to those lines was MORE
correct than my label for them.

So the corpus cannot settle this. It is mostly his testing noise, not
conversation, and the one genuinely real example - the Angie phone call from
his screenshot - was never captured to the repo. Offline scoring is blocked
on data that does not exist, not on effort.

The scorer is committed anyway, because it is correct and it will work the
moment real linked data exists. What it needs is parent_line verdicts
collected from real days, which is exactly what the shadow switch produces.

Nothing is claimed for links. They are not proven better and they are not
proven worse.
