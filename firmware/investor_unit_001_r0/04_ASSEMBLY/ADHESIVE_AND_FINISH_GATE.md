# PA12-to-PETG band bond and finish gate

The printed band is a structural adapter between two ordered nylon parts. A
nice seam is not proof of retention.

1. Confirm the ordered shell material is PA12/nylon and the printed band is the
   labeled PETG/PETG-HF lot.
2. Use a manufacturer TDS that explicitly supports the actual substrate pair.
   Permabond TA4550 is the documented target for nylon-to-PETG, but its local
   stock is unconfirmed. Do not improvise with hot glue, hobby cement, or
   electronics silicone.
3. Print at least three small PETG coupons with the same orientation/walls as
   the band. Bond them to a sacrificial spare ordered nylon shell, never the
   only usable body.
4. Tuesday morning prepare, mix, apply, and fixture exactly to the TDS. Record
   lot, expiry, room temperature, mix time, fixture time, working-strength time,
   and full-cure time.
5. At the TDS working-strength time, destructively pull/twist one coupon. If it
   passes and the nylon/PETG show no attack, use the same controlled process to
   bond the selected empty band to the Unit 001 body by noon Tuesday. Co-cure a
   second witness coupon beside it for the complete 24 hours.
6. After the full cure and before Wednesday assembly, destructively test the
   co-cured witness coupon with pliers and a guarded fixture.
   Reject clean adhesive peel, brittle flaking, material whitening, distortion,
   or solvent attack. Photograph the failure mode.
7. On Unit 001 use a thin controlled bond line only in the band/body retention
   land. Keep it out of hooks and internal electronics. Remove cosmetic squeeze
   out before cure using only a substrate-approved method.
8. After full cure: 100 firm hand shakes, six-direction 1 m drop test, USB
   handling, and moderate hand twist must not open, creep, crack, or whiten the
   seam. Reopen the removable face and inspect the cell afterward.

If the coupon or unit fails, do not hand it off. Change the joint process and
repeat every mechanical and closed-case test.
