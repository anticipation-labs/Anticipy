## THE ONE THING THAT BLOCKS BUILD 126 BEING USEFUL

Audit rows **F45, F48 and F49 are SHIP-BLOCKERS and were not in any fix
cluster.** All three are the same fact from three angles, and it is this:

**The Cloudflare brain does not serve the owner's own account.**

`ANTICIPY_SERVE_OWNERS` holds only `qeuy6sv1raof9rw` — the probe, a fictional
555 account, allowlisted *outside* the cap so `e2e_cloudflare.py` can run
without touching a real person. The config's own comment says, in as many
words, *never put a real owner here to skip the cap*. So real owners are
reached only through the cap, and the cap has been **1** on the last three
deploys. `plan.ts` spends the cap on discovered owners **in id order**, and the
lowest id of the five is another 555 test account. The single slot goes to a
fixture.

Why this blocks: build 126 posts to `api.anticipy.ai`, so a phone carrying it
lands its speech on D1. If the Cloudflare brain is not serving that owner,
**nobody hears them.** The line arrives, the row is written, and no brain ever
reads it. That is precisely the shape `overnight/are_the_ears_live.py` exists
to catch, and it exists because the ears went deaf for 30 hours and nothing
noticed.

The fix is one dispatch, and it is the owner's:

    gh workflow run "Brain → Cloudflare Containers (deploy)" \
      --ref cloudflare-backend -f confirm=DEPLOY -f cap=5

Five is the number of discoverable real-email owners today; two of the five are
555 fixtures and cost nothing. It was attempted in this session and refused by
the sandbox, correctly — turning the brain on for real people is a cutover
decision, not a deploy step.

**Why there is no duplicate-text risk in doing it.** Railway is still up
(HTTP 200) and still serves those owners. A phone on an old build posts to
Railway and is heard by the Railway brain; a phone on 126 posts to D1 and is
heard by the Cloudflare brain. Each line lands in exactly one place, so neither
brain sees the other's. The narrow exception is one owner running an old build
and 126 on two devices at once, which splits their day across two brains
without duplicating any single line.

**After the dispatch, prove it rather than assume it** (law 3): list the
container instances and confirm the intended owners are running, then have a
real phone on 126 speak one line and check that a decision lands on that row.
