## The one-line diagnosis

Anticipy has the rarest thing in a consumer app — a house voice that refuses to lie, a consent flow that conducts the permission moment instead of ambushing with it, and screens that pair a cost with the receipt for it — and then it spends that credibility on an opening sequence that asks for everything before it has demonstrated anything, and on a set of small false sentences that the same voice would never allow if anyone had walked the flow twice. The specific psychological problem is **ordering, not tone**: a stranger types an email, a password and a phone number (all three gate `canGo`, `AuthView.swift:349`) before the product has produced one thing of its own, is then counted at "1 of 4" as though the work they just did was worth nothing (`OnboardingView.swift:144`, `:176`), and lands on "Give me a day. You'll see." (`OnboardingFinale.swift:35`) even when they declined the microphone thirty seconds earlier. Compounding it, the app keeps five device-local mirrors of the owner across a sign-out (`AnticipyApp.swift:1257-1278`) and reads none of them back from the server, so the second person to use a handset is shown the first person's email at the door, their name in the tour, and their phone number wearing a confident "That's you" tick (`OnboardingView.swift:666`). Every one of those is a confident default that is wrong — which for an app asking to listen all day is not a conversion problem, it is a trust problem.

## The first-run problem

The traced order a brand-new person actually walks, with what each screen breaks.

**1. `AuthView` in `.signUp` — email + password + phone, all three required (`AuthView.swift:139-151`, gate at `:349`).**
- *Reciprocity.* Nothing has been produced yet. The screen does speak — "I'm Anticipy. Make an account and I'll start keeping your day for you." (`AuthView.swift:120`, `:129`) — but a sentence is not a sample, and `design/day-zero.md:25` (committed 2026-08-22, the newer of the two design docs) states outright: "Nothing in phase 1 asks for an account," under the heading "earn, then ask."
- *Contrast.* "It takes about ten seconds" (`:129`) is the only number a stranger is ever given, so it becomes the ruler for four beats plus two iOS alerts. `design/CONSUMER-FEEL-DIRECTION-2026-08-03.md:596-624` budgets the door alone at 0:00→0:20 and Home at 1:00, so the number understates its own screen by half.
- *Smart defaults, and this one is a disclosure bug.* `signOut()` leaves `ownerEmail` on the phone (`AnticipyApp.swift:249`, `:1257-1278`); `AuthView`'s `.task` reads it, prefills it, and flips to `.signIn` (`:79-88`), so the first screen a second person sees carries the previous owner's address under the title "Welcome back." (`:121`). `FirstRunOwnership.swift:6-9` calls a passed-along handset "the normal case, not the edge one."
- Right here already: the dialling code arrives from this phone, not from a US example (`AuthView.swift:151`, `DiallingCode.swift:5-16`); the three rule lines turn champagne as each is met and use the same predicate the button does (`:155-163`, `:331-333`).

**2. `welcome` — progress track deliberately hidden (`OnboardingView.swift:185-187`).**
- *Goal gradient.* The track behind it opens at "1 of 4" (`:144`, `:176`) and counts the account as zero, though the person just typed three fields including a phone number.
- *Repetition.* "I'm Anticipy." is said twice — `AuthView.swift:120` and again at `OnboardingView.swift:381-382`.

**3. `howItWorks` — "2 of 4".** Nothing wrong here.

**4. `micPrimer` — "3 of 4", then two iOS alerts (`:275-284`, `:557`).**

- Right here already: this is the highest-integrity screen in the flow. **5. `yourNumber` — "4 of 4".**
- *Smart defaults.* The repo specified this screen as a confirmation two documents ago — `CONSUMER-FEEL-DIRECTION-2026-08-03.md:615-616`: "she has it from the door … the step becomes a confirmation, not a second interrogation" — and half of it shipped: the seed is at `OnboardingView.swift:651-656` with a comment reading "Already told us at the door? **6. `EnrollmentInvite` — never raised on this build (`EnrollmentInvite.swift:29-39`).** It refuses to run when it cannot work and says so plainly. Leave it alone.

**7. `OnboardingFinale`.**
- *Loss aversion, inverted.* Someone who tapped "Not right now" — or who tapped yes and then denied the iOS alert, which `OnboardingView.swift:204` already branches on — gets the identical ending: "Give me a day. ## What the design system is missing

`Theme.swift` is 718 lines of colour, spacing, radii, springs, glass/ghost button surfaces, `CardBackground`/`anticipyCard` (`:369-414`), `GrainLayer` (`:335`), `LogoMark` (`:463`), `Haptics` (`:502-541`), `TypewriterText` (`:561`), `WaveBars` (`:639`), `PulseDot` (`:669`), `BreathingDot` (`:691`). What these fixes need and it does not have:

1. **A shared duration formatter.** `duration(_:)` is private at `ListeningDiagnosticsView.swift:223`, and fixes 8 and 9 both need it on other screens. Lift it into a small pure type alongside `ListenControlPolicy` — not into `Theme`, it is not a view — so three screens cannot word the same seconds differently. This is the single highest-value gap in the list, because the whole no-threshold argument (`ListeningDiagnosticsView.swift:38-43`) depends on the same seconds reading the same way everywhere.
2. **A confirmed-field row.** The filled row with the champagne tick lives inline at `OnboardingView.swift:661-670` and is needed again for email and phone in fix 10. There is no text-field component in `Theme` at all.
3. **A field-caption state machine** — neutral / valid / not-yet-valid / save-failed. The app has three phone fields (`AuthView.swift:139-151`, `OnboardingView.swift:616-677`, `SettingsView.swift:231-255`) with three different sets of states, which is exactly why fix 7 is a port rather than a one-liner. One `fieldCaption(state:)` would stop the drift structurally.
4. **A "verb — N noun" button label helper.** Fixes 5 and 6 both need singular/plural grammar branched on a count; `ContentView.swift:240` already does it inline in a third place. One helper, so the 1-vs-N wording is written once.
5. **An example/fixture treatment.** `.opacity + .allowsHitTesting(false) + accessibilityHidden` at `ContentView.swift:1468-1471` is ad hoc. An `.exampleContent(label:)` modifier makes "this is a sample, not your data" one thing that is correct for sighted and VoiceOver users at the same time.

What `Theme` must **not** gain: a progress bar, a meter, a percentage, or a generic parameterised step track. See below.

## What NOT to do

**The loss-aversion principle, applied literally, would destroy this product.** The source example is a countdown timer over a user's files with a dismiss button reading "I'll risk it" — the video itself calls that screen a threat. Anticipy asks for a person's microphone all day; a manufactured deadline, a guilt-worded dismiss, a red badge or a "you're missing out" line would contradict copy the app already ships and would be the end of the only asset it has. The rule for this repo: **a loss may be named only when the phone measured it, and it must be reported without a threshold, a colour or a verdict.** That is why fixes 8 and 9 are built on `ListenTally.unheardForSeconds` and copy `ListeningDiagnosticsView.swift:38-43` — "a phone that has heard nothing for eleven hours and a phone that has heard nothing for four minutes both say so, and the reader judges." Every escape hatch in the app today ("Not right now" `OnboardingView.swift:219`, "I'll do this later" `ContentView.swift:267`, "Not now" `ContextAskSheet.swift:96-105`) is full-width, real size, and unguilty. None of the fixes above touch one, and none ever should.

**Smart defaults stop at the consent line.** Pre-selecting the common choice is right for a phone field and wrong for a microphone or a context grant. Nothing in this list pre-checks a permission, and nothing should.

**Reciprocity does not mean unlocking the fixtures.** The empty state's example cards are not a held-back result and must not be raised to full opacity (fix 20) — a first-timer reading a fixture as a real job is a worse failure than the one being fixed.

Specific rejected recommendations, with the pattern behind why they died:

- **"Add an interview question to the mic screen."** First run has been closed to additions three times in writing — `OnboardingView.swift:58-67`, `EnrollmentInvite.swift:18-25`, and `day-zero.md:30-33` ("this phase spends all of it, so nothing may be added here"). It is also the wrong screen: a text box about someone's bank, stacked under four promises at the moment they decide whether to hand over the microphone, is a second decision competing with the one that matters (`day-zero.md:232`, "Consent is a rule list, never four cards").
- **"Say how much of today she missed when listening is off" (`ContentView.swift:1243`).** Both branches are unreachable there. `.stoppedByOwner` hard-zeroes `unheardForSeconds` at `ListenTally.swift:317` on purpose — `:58-60` says why: "Quiet afterwards is the ordinary state of a phone nobody is talking to, not a finding." `.stoppedByOther` never reaches that line because `offLine` renders only under `!isListening` (`ContentView.swift:1198`) and interruption leaves `isListening` true (`PhoneListener.swift:86`). The instinct was right; it survives as fix 9, at the line where the state is reachable.
- **"Offer tappable option chips on her questions" (`ContentView.swift:1658`).** `AskCard`'s own doc comment (`:1622-1632`) rejects it: "a proactive check-in dressed up as an approval would teach him to stop reading them." Chips are approval grammar, and `AskCard` also renders `clock` — the proactive check-in, where authored options are Yes/No, i.e. a consent dialog on the one card that must not be one. `docs/BRIEF.html:216` makes free text first-class on purpose.
- **"Give the battery number a second number."** The premise is false — `batteryWording` already pairs the spend with its window and argues that pairing at `ListeningDiagnosticsView.swift:149-155`. Current charge is a state, not a cost over the measured window; putting them side by side invites a rate that is not true, particularly since `batteryMeasuredSeconds` can span several charge cycles.
- **"Build a parameterised step track in `Theme.swift:639`."** Onboarding rejected that shape with the reasoning written down (`OnboardingView.swift:157-161`, `:185-187`), a first-run person has finished nothing so the "done" parameter is permanently 0 there, and the voice meter should stay a `ProgressView` — leaving one real caller, which fix 6 handles in place. Shared infrastructure with one caller, built by overriding a documented decision at the only other caller, is speculative.

The pattern in every kill: **the codebase had already made the decision, in a comment or a design doc, with an argument.** Read the comment above the line before changing the line.
