# Round-2 research notes (own dive, beyond first 24 findings)

## AI-content trust penalty (directly validates founder instinct)
- J. Interactive Advertising 2025 (N=304): AI disclosure → ↑persuasion knowledge → ↓trust in ad AND organization.
- JRIM 2025 (N=358, Prolific, ChatGPT/StableDiffusion stimuli): AI disclosure diminishes ad value & purchase intent, mediated by credibility. Luo et al. 2019: chatbot AI-disclosure before conversation significantly reduces purchase rates.
- Adm. Sci. 2025 (3 studies): informed consumers rate human-made images significantly higher, d = 0.52. Motive matters: "cost efficiency" motive → significant trust & purchase-intent decline; "privacy protection" motive → parity with human images.
- MDPI JTAER 2024: perceived eeriness of AI ads ↓acceptance; "synthesis" (visible compositing) drives eeriness; verisimilitude reduces it.
- Application: AI-detectable visuals are quantifiably costly. Anything that looks synthesized (warped geometry, impossible physics, AI faces) has measurable purchase-intent cost, not just aesthetic. Use footage seeded from real product; avoid faces; if AI-generated visuals are used, they must be indistinguishable OR motivated.

## Scrollytelling (validates LiveDemo architecture)
- ACM 2023 (long-form journalism study): scrollytelling significantly ↑perceived engagement + emotional response vs static; comprehension preserved.
- CHI 2026 privacy-policy scrollytelling (N=454): ↑engagement, ↓cognitive load, ↑perceived clarity, comprehension matched.
- NYT finding: readers ignored steppers/tabs/sliders — "they just wanted to scroll" → scroll-triggered animation is the right interaction primitive.
- Application: scroll-scrubbed LiveDemo is the evidence-backed format; avoid click-to-advance interactions.

## Mental imagery / touch simulation
- JFMM 2022 (N=179): product visuals that trigger mental simulation for touch → ↑product attitudes (fully mediated by simulation). Macro texture shots (brushed titanium grain) do persuasion work, not decoration.
- JMIS 2019: visual presentation facilitating mental imagery of touching ↑purchase.

## IKEA effect / customization
- Meta-analysis 2025 (k=55, N=5,454): self-assembly/creation labor → valuation d = 0.57; robust; secondary: liking, ownership.
- Franke, Schreier, Kaiser "I designed it myself" (Mgmt Sci 2010): self-design premium in WTP, mediated by feeling of accomplishment; preference fit alone doesn't explain.
- Application: even a tiny configuration choice (chain length, engraving) measurably raises valuation & ownership pre-purchase.

## Color/status for premium
- JCR 2025 (7 studies): LOW color saturation → ↑perceived luxury status (mediated by continuity heritage) — but REVERSED for brands positioned as innovative. Anticipy is innovation-positioned → muted palette good, but pair with explicit newness framing carefully.
- Psych & Marketing 2021 (11 experiments): darker colors → perceived higher hierarchy/dominance; Hagtvedt 2019: dark = durable, light = user-friendly.
- Application: near-black + graphite palette scientifically consistent with premium/durable; the gold accent = the single signal color.

## Goal gradient / endowed progress
- Kivetz et al. JMR 2006: effort accelerates near goal; endowed progress (2 pre-stamped of 12) → faster completion than 0-of-10.
- Application: waitlist→reserve ladder; purchase flow should show progress ("step 2 of 3 — almost yours"); post-reserve timeline gives endowed progress toward August.

## Queues/waitlists as value signals
- Koo & Fishbach JMR 2010: standing in line ↑product value.
- Imas & Madarász RESTUD 2024: desire increases in how much others want-but-can't-have it ("superiority seeking") — theoretical basis for honest exclusivity.
- Luxury queue study 2025: queues signal status but poorly managed ones damage loyalty.
- Application: a real waitlist count or batch structure (if true) increases value; never fabricate.

## Social proof / eWOM effect sizes
- JMR 2016 meta-analysis (1,532 effect sizes, 96 studies): eWOM–sales correlation avg .091; VOLUME beats valence; effect stronger for tangible goods NEW to market (= pre-order hardware!); high review variability hurts more than negativity.
- J. Retailing 2014 meta (443 elasticities): valence elasticity .78, volume .41; stronger for high-involvement products; stronger on third-party sites.
- Application: with zero reviews, the priority once ANY buyers exist is collecting/broadcasting volume of authentic mentions; meanwhile founder+press+community substitutes.

## Pricing structure
- Partitioned pricing (Morwitz et al.): separated surcharges lower recalled total & raise demand — BUT Baymard: surprise costs at checkout = #1 abandonment cause. Net: free shipping folded in ("Free shipping US & Canada") is right for premium positioning.
- Default effects meta (n=73,675): d=0.68; strongest when default reads as endorsement or endowment. Application: pre-select the standard configuration; default to the sensible choice everywhere in checkout.
- Choice overload metas (Scheibehenne 2010; Chernev 2015): overload appears when options are complex/similar and buyer lacks expertise → one product, few options is an advantage; do NOT multiply SKUs.
- Decoy/compromise effects exist but require 3 options; single-product page can ignore; the "$199 at launch" acts as the only reference point needed.

## Free shipping
- TFS research: delivery timeliness & fairness perception matter; free shipping perceived as fair value signal for premium goods.

## Anticipation as product value (reframes the Aug-2026 wait)
- Kumar, Killingsworth & Gilovich 2014 (Psych Science, 4 studies incl. experience-sampling): anticipating EXPERIENTIAL purchases is pleasurable ("savoring"); anticipating material goods is impatient. Loewenstein 1987: people pay a premium to savor anticipation of good experiences.
- Kumar 2016 JCP: people prefer "have now, do later" — waiting for experiences generates utility; waiting for objects doesn't.
- Application: frame Anticipy as an EXPERIENCE (the feeling of a closed day, kept word) not a gadget shipment → converts the pre-order wait from a cost into savoring. Copy: what your life is like starting August. Post-purchase drip should feed anticipation (build updates = operational transparency too).
- Payment depreciation (Gourville & Soman 1998): paying well before consumption decouples pain of paying — by August the $149.99 feels sunk/free → pre-payment actually reduces later friction; also argues full payment now (vs deposit) increases commitment/lower cancellations.

## Pennies-a-day (Gourville JCR 1998)
- Temporal reframing to small daily equivalents raises compliance ($149.99+first year ≈ "40 cents a day for a year of kept promises") — works when the daily comparison retrieves small trivial expenses. Optional, use with care against fluency.

## Warranty/guarantee as quality signal
- Boulding & Kirmani JCR 1993: warranties read as quality signals mainly when the firm seems credible; Innis & Unnava 1991: warranties are MORE useful for new/unknown brands than reputable ones.
- Application: full-refund-before-shipping is not just risk reversal — it is a quality signal, and it works hardest precisely for an unknown brand like Anticipy. State it prominently and unconditionally.

## Anticipated regret (the honest FOMO)
- Simonson JCR 1992: asking people to anticipate regret shifts choices toward the action they'd regret missing less.
- I&M 2026 (2 experiments): anticipated INACTION regret (regret of not buying) drives purchase, amplified by scarcity cues; action regret suppressed by risk reversal.
- Application: the honest structure is: refund policy kills action-regret; true launch-price deadline creates inaction-regret. Copy like "In August you'll either wear it or wish you'd reserved it" is a legitimate inaction-regret prompt (no fake scarcity needed).

## Mobile funnel data
- 117-brand / 486M-session dataset: mobile = ~78% of traffic, converts ~1.56x worse; the leak is at CHECKOUT (62.4% vs 50.5% abandonment), not browsing. Friction sources: typing-heavy forms, forced accounts, non-mobile payment methods, load time (>3s penalty).
- Application: most Anticipy visitors will be mobile (esp. from IG/reels). Mobile purchase flow deserves as much care as the desktop story; add Apple Pay/Google Pay (one-tap = the single biggest mobile checkout fix); keep video payloads small.

## Case study: Pebble
- 275k pre-orders; tagline "E-Paper Watch for iPhone and Android" — maximal concreteness; first sentence = the problem; avg pledge ~$150 (Anticipy's exact price point — validated willingness to pay for wearables at $149).
- Lesson: clarity of what-it-is beat style; community + build updates sustained the long wait (anticipation feeding, operational transparency).

## Page length
- Dvir & Gafni 2018 (large-scale web experiments, n≈535k+ visits): SHORTER landing pages converted significantly better for low-commitment conversions (email capture), across geos/time. Caveat: their conversion = lead-gen, not $150 purchase; high-price/high-involvement purchases still benefit from complete objection handling (ELM central route). Resolution: length is fine IF every screen earns attention (emotion→comprehension→proof→objections→close); nothing padded.

## Speed = conversion (motion budget constraint)
- Deloitte/Google "Milliseconds Make Millions" (37 brands, 4 weeks, US+EU): a 0.1s mobile speed improvement → retail conversions +8.4%, AOV +9.2%; luxury engagement +8.6%.
- Application: heavy autoplay video/GSAP payloads have a measurable conversion COST that must be netted against their persuasive benefit. Compress aggressively, lazy-load below-fold clips, poster frames first.

## Delay/trust management (post-purchase, protects refund rate)
- CSCW 2017 (interviews + regression on 4,089 delayed Kickstarter projects): backers' trust during delays hinges on creator COMMUNICATION during delay, creator background research-ability, and delay duration.
- JBV 2024 (155 updates, 17,807 backer comments, LIWC): four practices tamp down negativity — frequent communication, evidence of progress, proximal temporal reach ("next month" not "eventually"), time-quality tradeoff framing; diminishing returns if delays continue.
- Application: commit to a visible build-updates cadence between reserve and August; makes ship-date risk credible/managed, feeds anticipation, prevents refunds.

## Founder narrative
- UT Austin dissertation (96 campaigns + N=457 experiment): "mini-narratives" — short founder-narrated problem→solution stories — outperform both no narrative and overwrought narrative.
- INSEAD: novice entrepreneurs mobilize more resources when narrating EXPERIMENTATION (what they tested/learned) vs pure planning.
- Application: founder note should be a 3-sentence problem→solution mini-story + one concrete thing learned building it; not a bio, not a manifesto.

## Bystander privacy (the social objection nobody words)
- CHI 2026 (N=525 + 20 interviews): bystanders demand much stronger transparency than wearers will give; 65–90% take defensive action in sensitive contexts. Denning CHI 2014: acceptability hinges on visible recording state and asking permission.
- Application: buyers imagine wearing it AROUND OTHER PEOPLE — the site must answer "what do I say when my friend asks if it's recording?" (visible listening indicator, one-tap mute, no audio stored, transcripts only, etc. — whatever is factually true). This social-script objection is distinct from personal data privacy and currently unaddressed.

## Anthropomorphism / human tone
- ISR field experiment (chatbot buy-back): human-like features ↑ transaction conversion — but ↑ price-fairness sensitivity. Human voice/tone helps; keep offers clean and fair-feeling.
