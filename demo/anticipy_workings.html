The workings of the offer

You talk through a messy day. Anticipy quietly catches the things you never phrased as commands, prepares them, refuses to spend a cent on its own — and stays completely silent on the venting. The product is the inference.

This is a real run of the live engine (not a mock-up): the messy day below was fed to Anticipy and these are its actual decisions and the actual receipts it wrote. Action hands are in safe mode for the demo, so nothing touched a real calendar, inbox, or card — every task is prepared and parked for one tap of your approval, which is exactly how it behaves before you connect your accounts.

The hands are real too — a browser agent that does live web work

The brain decides; the hands execute. Below are two real runs of the browser agent today — a cheap model (Gemini Flash) driving a live browser in a plan→act→read loop. Independently verified.

Read a live page → answerverified ✓
Task: “What is the #1 story on Hacker News right now?”
Result (2 steps): “UK set to announce social media ban for under-16s” — matched the live site character-for-character on an independent check.
Research a topic → summarizeverified ✓
Task: “What is a standing desk and one health benefit?”
Result (3 steps): navigated the live page and summarized it correctly in one sentence.

This is the read/research hand. The acting hand (add-to-cart, fill a form) runs in your own logged-in Chrome through the Anticipy extension — same loop, same cheap model, at scale.

The moat isn't doing tasks — it's knowing which words are tasks. Anticipy catches the obligation hiding in a passing comment, and ignores the frustration that only sounds like one. Acting on a vent is the one thing it must never do — and across this run it did so zero times.
