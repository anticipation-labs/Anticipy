Anticipy should work like a helpful person who remembers the thread of your day.

Your phone hears something, or you type or text it. The system saves the exact words and their context. Then the brain decides whether anything needs doing. Saying, the kids are already on the calendar, is different from asking to add them. A reply like yes only makes sense beside the question it answers.

The harness is everything that makes that reasoning useful: memory, account connections, task storage, permissions, tools, and checking what actually happened.

The tools can read a connected app, use a paired browser, or ask your phone to update its calendar. A task sitting in a queue is not proof that the work happened. Anticipy needs to check the result and bring it back to you.

The overnight work found real failures: a broken SendBlue webhook configuration, a reproduced interface freeze, keyboard overlap, oversized cards, and replies that were generated and then discarded.
