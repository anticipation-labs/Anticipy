# CRITIQUE — completeness and correctness review of the Cloudflare migration plan

Reviewed 2026-09-03 against the tree at `migration/` on branch
`cloudflare-migration`. Every claim below was checked against the file it
names. Where I ran something, the command and its output are given.

**Verdict up front:** the *documents* are strong — `CUTOVER.md`, `WEBSITE.md`,
`RULES.md`, `GAPS.md`, `SECRETS.md` and `EXPORT.md` are usable, measured, and
honest about what they could not verify. The *code* is a skeleton that a
reader of those documents will mistake for an implementation. Four of
twenty-six collections exist. Zero of fifty-five routes exist. Two of the six
`routerUse` middlewares are missing entirely. Three record-level hooks with
retention and uniqueness semantics have no home. And the port introduces at
least one authorization regression that PocketBase's own rules were preventing.

The plan is safe to *start*. It is not safe to reach Phase 6 on, and the
reason it is not is not written down anywhere in it.

## 1.4 BLOCKER — three record-level hooks have no home, and one of them is a uniqueness invariant

These are `onRecordCreate` / `onRecordAfterCreateSuccess`, not HTTP hooks. They
fire on *every* write path — HTTP, cron, the dashboard, the worker. `records.ts`
has no hook dispatch of any kind.

1. **`job_commitment_identity.pb.js:10-19`** clears `commitment_key` when a job
   goes `done|failed|cancelled`. `schema.sql:298-304` is explicit about why:
   `idx_jobs_active_commitment` is a partial-unique index and *"this is the
   ONLY thing that stops two processes both reading 'no active promise' and
   both creating one."* Drop the hook and every terminal job holds its
   commitment identity forever, so the legitimate retry that the index is
   designed to allow is refused permanently. Worse: `commitment_key` is not in
   `schema.ts`'s `jobs` column map at all (§2.1), so a client that writes it
   gets a 400 and the invariant is unreachable from both directions.

2. **`audit_retention.pb.js:71-83`** — the standing 300-row cap on
   `agent_llm_audit`. `1700000037_backup_footprint.js:13-14` records that this
   table filled the 5 GB volume and took production down. Its `*_json` columns
   hold up to 1 MB each (`RULES.md`, `1700000032:11`).

3. **`evidence.pb.js:244-269`** — 20 screenshots per owner, 60 total, swept 25
   at a time. In the new design the *bytes* move to R2 and **nothing prunes
   R2**. The row cap was also the privacy control ("nobody's screenshots
   accumulate indefinitely just because they were the quiet account",
   `:241-243`). Both halves are gone.

## 1.5 HIGH — account signup is broken, and the "fix" corrupts accounts

`clients/ios/Anticipy/Backend/AnticipyBackend.swift:442-449` POSTs
`{email, password, passwordConfirm, legacy_uuid, phone?}` to
`/api/collections/owners/records`.

On the Worker: `guard.ts:98` lets it through (rung 3, correctly). Then
`records.create` (`records.ts:163-176`) rejects any key not in the column map —
`passwordConfirm` is not — and returns **400 `unknown_field`**. Signup is dead.

The obvious repair is worse than the bug. Add `passwordConfirm` to the map and
the row is written with `password` = the **plaintext** (`toColumn` at

`auth.ts:158-159` returns `null` for an empty `tokenKey`, so the account can
never authenticate — silently, forever. There is no `createOwner` path in
`auth.ts`; the file's only writers are `rotateTokenKey` and `setPassword`, and
neither is called from anywhere.

`RULES.md` (owners row) says the open `createRule` is *"deliberate and
load-bearing: `1700000013` exists because production had `null` here and every
signup returned 403 while the login screen looked fine."* The port reproduces
the outage the migration was written to fix.

## 1.7 HIGH — nothing extracts the password hashes from the archive

The plan names this as its own top blocker in four places
(`EXPORT.md:221`, `:644`, `export_pocketbase.sh:37`, `import_d1.py:217-219`)
and implements it in **zero**.

`export_pocketbase.sh:438-449` does download the native archive — good. But

> `this export has NO native PocketBase archive. Password hashes
> (owners.password), tokenKeys and agents.agent_token are NOT in it.`

That warning fires when the archive is *absent*. When the archive is
**present**, nothing reads it. `grep -rn "FROM owners\|data.db" runbooks/` finds
no SQL that pulls `password`, `tokenKey` or `agent_token` out of
`pb_data/data.db` and merges them into the D1 load. There is no

that are empty, and every customer is locked out and every browser unpaired at
cutover. This is the exact failure the plan predicted, left un-fixed.

**Concretely, this is what the runbook is missing:**

```sh

sqlite3 -json "$tmp/pb_data/data.db" \
  "SELECT id, password, tokenKey FROM owners;" > secrets/owners_auth.json
sqlite3 -json "$tmp/pb_data/data.db" \
  "SELECT id, agent_token FROM agents;"        > secrets/agent_tokens.json
# import_d1.py needs a --auth-overlay flag that merges these by id, the same
# way it already merges the vault rewrap (import_d1.py:589, :665-667, :699).
```

The vault rewrap already has exactly this shape. Reuse it.

# 2. WHAT IS WRONG

## 2.1 BLOCKER — `schema.ts` contradicts `schema.sql` on three of its four tables

I diffed the column maps programmatically against the DDL:

```
jobs    in SQL not in TS: commitment_key, device_id, reconciliation, source_event_ids
agents  in SQL not in TS: llm_calls, llm_hour, solve_calls, solve_hour
events  in SQL not in TS: backfill, boot_id, decision, device_id, explicit,
                          external_event_id, gap_before_ms, needs_confirmation,
                          parent_line, segment, seq
events  in TS not in SQL: intent, memory_purged
owners  clean
```

Two distinct failure modes, both fatal to the data path:

**(a) Columns in TS that do not exist in the database.** `schema.ts:72`
declares `intent: T`; `schema.ts:73` declares `memory_purged: B`. Neither is a
column of `events` — `memory_purged` belongs to `purges` (`schema.sql:575`) and
`intent` exists nowhere. `records.create` will emit
`INSERT INTO "events" (…, "intent") VALUES (…)`, SQLite raises
`no such column: intent`, and there is no try/catch around
`records.ts:179-182` — the request 500s with a raw D1 error.

**(b) Columns in the database that are missing from TS.**
`schema.sql:221` declares `"device_id" TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT ''
CHECK (length("device_id") > 0)` — **required**. It is not in `schema.ts`, so
`records.create` returns `400 {"device_id":{"code":"unknown_field"}}`. **Every
event write from the phone and the pendant is refused.** That is the primary
data path of the product. `seq`, `segment`, `boot_id`, `capture_*` and
`external_event_id` (the Twilio idempotency key, `schema.sql:244-245`) go the
same way.

`agents.llm_calls/llm_hour/solve_calls/solve_hour` are the per-agent rate-limit
counters written by `agent_key.pb.js` and `captcha_solve.pb.js`. Absent from
the map, the LLM proxy cannot meter itself.

`tsc --noEmit` passes on all of this, which is the point: the column map is
plain data and nothing type-checks it against the DDL. That is precisely why
`schema.ts:9-13` says it must be generated — and the generator does not exist.

## 2.3 BLOCKER — the service token becomes strictly more powerful than it is today

This is the structural error and it is the one that will not be caught by
tests, because the tests are written against the current code.

In PocketBase the guard is a *filter in front of* the rules, not a replacement
for them. `guard.pb.js:37` returns `e.next()` for the service token; PocketBase
then evaluates the collection's own rule. `RULES.md` counts **14 `null` slots
in the product half** and **70 in the HQ half** (all 5 rules × 14 collections).
`null` means superuser-only, and a service-token holder is not a superuser. So
today `X-Anticipy-Token` cannot read `password_resets`, cannot PATCH or DELETE
`evidence`, cannot delete an `owners` row, and cannot touch a single
`internal_*` table.

On the Worker, `guard.ts:84` is `if (ctx.principal.kind === "service") return
null;` and **there is no second layer.** `grep -rn "listRule\|deleteRule\|
updateRule\|createRule\|serviceOnly" migration/workers/src/` finds only
comments. Once `schema.ts` is generated over all 26 tables — which is the
stated plan — a single header grants full CRUD on the company password vault,
the expense ledger, the HQ notes, the reset-code hashes, and the ability to
`DELETE /api/collections/owners/records/{id}` straight past
`account_delete.pb.js:57` and its `purges` row.

**This must be a data structure, not prose.** Something like:

```ts
// src/pb/rules.ts — the 60 product + 70 HQ rule slots, as code.
export const RULE: Record<string, Record<"list"|"view"|"create"|"update"|"delete", Rung>> = {
  evidence:        { list: "guard", view: "guard", create: "guard",
                     update: "superuser", delete: "superuser" },  // 1700000045:88-89
  password_resets: { list: "superuser", view: "superuser", create: "superuser",
                     update: "superuser", delete: "superuser" },  // 1700000012:34-38
  internal_notes:  { list: "superuser", …, delete: "superuser" }, // 1700000052 — all null
  …
};
```
…checked *after* the guard says yes and *before* `records.ts` runs. Without it,
`RULES.md` is 191 lines of specification with no implementation, and the 84
`null` slots that are the strongest posture in the product are deleted by
migration.

## 2.4 HIGH — a signed-in account can re-open an expired evidence share window

Directly downstream of §2.3, and worth calling out on its own because
`guard.pb.js` says in so many words that it is impossible.

`guard.pb.js:410-415`:

> *"the collection's null update/delete rules mean the widening cannot become a
> way to edit or erase a receipt even though this branch would permit the
> request."*

`guard.ts:225-231` is the port of that branch and it permits the request. There
is nothing behind it. So an account holder can

```
PATCH /api/collections/evidence/records/<own id>
{"share_expires":"2099-01-01T00:00:00Z","fetches":0}
```

and mint a permanent public URL for a screenshot of a page they were logged
into — defeating `assets.ts:110-133`, `evidence.pb.js:157-223`, the 15-minute
window and the 5-fetch ceiling in one request. `RULES.md`'s evidence row says
the `null` rules **are** "the whole security model of the collection."

Same shape, smaller blast radius: `RULES.md` prescribes "delete: service token
only" for `events`, `jobs`, `agents`, `segments` and `pendants`, and
"update/delete: service token only" for `events`. `guard.ts:225-231` is
method-agnostic and allows all of them for the owning account.

EXISTS` vs an existing table: **the second one silently no-ops and you get
whichever schema ran first.** Which one that is, is not specified anywhere.

- `records.create` never applies `forcedScope`. Safe *today* only because
  `guard.ts:224` requires `b.owner_ref === authId` on the create path. If the
  rule table of §2.3 is added, put the scope on the INSERT too.

# 3. WHAT IS DANGEROUSLY OPTIMISTIC

## 3.1 The `guard.pb.js` port is good. The claim that it is *complete* is not.

Rung by rung, `guard.ts` is faithful: the service token by constant-time
compare (`:145`), the terminal agent rung with the unresolved-credential
refusal (`:88-94`, matching `guard.pb.js:203-220`), the four evidence columns
(`:65`, `:145-153` vs `guard.pb.js:261-273`), the `read_line`/`read_fact`
400-character door with the `watching_until` check (`:158-170` vs `:297-323`),
the superuser-above-account ordering (`:101`), the pre-owner pair-code branch
(`:203-207`), the blank-owner ban (`:212-215`), the `perPage > 50` cap
(`:288`), the `owner_ref`-in-body refusal on the tokenless claim (`:309-312`).
The `503` on a missing service token (`:78-81`) is a genuine improvement over
`guard.pb.js:25-26`'s fail-open, and the AST scope injection (`:135`, `:220`,
`:228`) genuinely closes the live bypass at `guard.pb.js:45-50`.

What is optimistic is the surrounding claim that porting the guard ports the
authorization. It does not, because in PocketBase the guard is one of two
layers and the second one — 84 `null` rule slots — has no port at all (§2.3,
§2.4). `chain.ts:5-26` states the danger perfectly ("If this chain is skipped,
mis-ordered, or shipped disabled, every transcript… is readable by anyone who
can guess a URL") and then implements only the half it was looking at.

## 3.2 `research_lane` drops one leg and mis-scopes another

Legs 1-5 are transcribed carefully, including the exhaustive field-by-field
`isResearchHandback` (`research_lane.ts:194-229` vs
`research_lane.pb.js:509-543`) and the device-lane shape refusals. The AST
`mentionsField` replacement for `/\blane\b/` (`research_lane.ts:63-65`) is a
real fix for a real fail-open.

Two problems:

- **The approval-rewrite legs at `research_lane.pb.js:612-632` are folded into
  the create/claim branches (`research_lane.ts:105-124`) but the file's own
  §separate case — a 403 "that explains itself" for a body that writes
  `approval` on a row it is about to run — is compressed into one condition.
  The 403 bodies differ from the originals at `:618` and `:626`, which the
  contract diff will surface as a mismatch on the safety path.**
- `researchLane` sets `ctx.extraAst` for the lane exclusions
  (`research_lane.ts:63-77`), and `buildWhere` (`records.ts:294-298`) compiles
  it. But `records.view`, `records.update` and `records.remove` **ignore
  `extraAst` entirely** — they only apply `forcedScope`. The deployed rewrite
  is list-only too, so this matches. Worth stating; it is not obviously safe
  and nothing says it was checked.

## 3.3 "Freeze writes on PocketBase" leaves the brain writing into a frozen database

`CUTOVER.md` 6.4 is five numbered steps: announce, freeze, final export,
repoint DNS, watch. It does not mention `ANTICIPY_PB` and it does not mention
the Twilio webhook.

`brain/worker.py:43` and `brain/supervisor.py:29` read
`ANTICIPY_PB`. `brain/` runs on Railway until Phase **7**. So between 6.4 step
2 and Phase 7, the brain is pointed at a read-only PocketBase: no jobs are
minted, no SMS replies are sent, and nothing in the runbook says so.

Worse, `brain/worker.py:885-911` refuses to start when `ANTICIPY_PB` and the
derived Twilio webhook origin disagree:

> *"TWO SERVICES DISAGREE about where … Unset the pin to use the derived URL,
> or point ANTICIPY_PB at …"*

So the repair is not "set one env var" — it is set `ANTICIPY_PB`, set
`ANTICIPY_TWILIO_WEBHOOK_URL`, and update the Twilio console, **in the same
window**, or the brain will not boot. `VERIFICATION.md:147-152` knows about the
Twilio-signs-the-full-URL problem and puts it on a checklist; `CUTOVER.md` 6.4
does not put it in the ordered steps, which is where it has to be.

## 3.4 "Firmware — already correct" is not established

`CLIENTS.md:120` and §2.5 (`:215-223`) declare the pendant **N/A** for this
migration because `firmware/src/config.h:61` is already
`API_BASE_URL "https://anticipy.ai"`. Three things wrong with that:

1. **It is the apex, not `www`.** `firmware/src/network.cpp:244,258` does
   `http.begin(client, API_TRANSCRIBE_URL); http.POST((uint8_t*)samples, …)`
   against `https://anticipy.ai/api/engine/transcribe` with up to 320 KB of
   audio (`config.h:57-58`). `CUTOVER.md:352-358` records that the apex today
   **307s** to `www` — and `CUTOVER.md:364-365` prescribes rebuilding it as a
   **301**. 307 preserves the method and body; 301 is the classic
   method-downgrade, and Arduino `HTTPClient` does not follow redirects at all
   by default. Either the pendant's uploads are already failing, or this plan
   step breaks them. Nobody checked which.
2. **`API_OTA_CHECK_PATH "/api/firmware/latest"` (`config.h:63`) has no route.**
   `ls src/app/api/` — there is no `firmware` directory. The pendant's own
   update channel points at a 404, which contradicts `CLIENTS.md:120`'s
   "DFU over BLE" and means the "lesson" the document draws from firmware is
   drawn from an endpoint list nobody verified.
3. The firmware is the one client with **no** update path in the plan at all.
   Declaring it N/A removes it from the Phase 8 tail measurement.

## 3.5 The contract suite can only report on what exists

165 test functions against a Worker that serves 4 of 26 collections and 0 of 55
routes will produce a diff that is mostly "404 vs 200". `CUTOVER.md` 6.1's
instruction to *"count the passes, not the exit code"* is right and
insufficient — the gate also needs a **coverage floor**: a per-collection and
per-route matrix asserting that every one of the 26 tables and 55 routes was
exercised at least once against both backends. Otherwise a suite that is 100 %
identical on the four implemented collections looks like a clean diff.

# 4. IS THE SEQUENCE SAFE?

Mostly yes, and the ordering instincts are good: DNS alone in its own phase,
Vercel kept as the rollback target (`:386`, `:402`), scale-to-zero for 30 days
before deletion (`:545-552`), *"verify a restore, do not verify a backup"*
(`:554`). Phase 0.2 — list the R2 backup bucket by hand before anything — is
the right first step and it is correctly marked as the single most important
one.

Five sequencing defects:

## 4.1 Phase 1.3 cannot be done in Phase 1

"Move the evidence host → R2" is listed at low risk with rollback "unset
`ANTICIPY_PUBLIC_URL`". But `ANTICIPY_PUBLIC_URL` only changes the **origin** of
the URL that `evidence.pb.js:220` hands to Twilio:

```js
url: base + "/api/files/evidence/" + rec.id + "/" + file,
```

The **bytes** are still in PocketBase's `/pb_data/storage`. Point that variable
at a Cloudflare host in Phase 1 and every `MediaUrl` 404s — and per
`evidence.pb.js:169-173`, a `MediaUrl` that 404s **fails the whole SMS**, not
just the picture. Actually moving the host requires the Worker, R2, the
`/api/files` route (missing, §1.2) and a blob copy, all of which are Phase 6.
Phase 1.3 is either a no-op or an outage. It is also listed as the phase's
highest-value move, so it will be attempted.

## 4.2 Phase 1.1 is already done and will fail

`git rm -r public/images/originals_backup` (`CUTOVER.md:112`). `ls
public/images/` shows no `originals_backup`; `du -sh public` is **44M**, not the
67M at `:108`. The directory was deleted by a concurrent commit. Minor, but it
is the first command in the first phase that touches the repo, and an operator
who hits `fatal: pathspec did not match` on step one loses confidence in every
step after it.

## 4.3 The two documents use the same phase numbers for different things

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §12 numbers **build** phases 0-7. `CUTOVER.md` numbers
**cutover** phases 0-8. They collide:

| N | `ARCHITECTURE.md` §12 | `CUTOVER.md` |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | writes + the safety system | website on a preview URL |
| 5 | **the 55 routes, R2, cron, HQ** | website on the real domain |
| 6 | cutover | the backend |
| 7 | decommission | the brain |

And the code cross-references them bare. `llm.ts:27` — *"the thing to test
before Phase 5"*. `ARCHITECTURE.md:630` — same. An operator working
`CUTOVER.md` reaches Phase 5 (DNS flip for the website), sees Phase 6's gate,
and has no way to know the 55 routes were supposed to be built at a *different*
Phase 5. **Rename one set.** Build phases → `B0…B7`.

## 4.4 The Phase 8 tail gate is unreachable as written

Condition 2 is *"zero real-client requests for 14 consecutive days"* on the
Railway hostname. `CLIENTS.md` §2.3 records that the Chrome extension is
self-hosted and **loaded unpacked — it never auto-updates**, and §2.2 that the
Mac app is a hand-built zip. A user who never revisits the download page keeps
the old URL forever. So the gate is honest and correct, and it will never go
green, which means Railway runs indefinitely and the "irreversible" phase never
arrives.

`CLIENTS.md` §4.4 offers "the one remote lever you do have" — the
`/agent/relocate` consumer. That is the actual mechanism for reaching zero and
it should be a **Phase 3 deliverable with its own go/no-go line**, not a
subsection. As written, the plan's terminal gate depends on a feature the plan
mentions but does not schedule.

## 4.5 Nothing irreversible happens before verification — one exception

Checked all eight phases: 8.2's ordering (scale to zero → 30 days → snapshot →
**restore the snapshot** → then delete) is genuinely careful. The one
irreversible-before-verified step is **Phase 2.4, the nameserver move**, whose
own rollback is "up to 48h of propagation." The plan knows (`:241-243`,
`:19`). Given that its only purpose is to enable Workers custom domains, which
are not needed until Phase 5, **Phase 2 should move to immediately before
Phase 5** — shrinking the window in which the zone is on new nameservers and
nothing has yet been proven on them, and keeping mail on the known-good
nameservers for the whole of Phases 3-4.

# 5. CAN THE OWNER STOP HALFWAY?

**Phases 0-5: yes, cleanly.** This is the plan's best property and it is
deliberate (`CUTOVER.md:27-29`: *"Phases 1-5 do not touch PocketBase"*). Stop
after Phase 5 and you have the website on Workers, DNS on Cloudflare, a stable
`api.anticipy.ai` in front of Railway, and PocketBase untouched. Vercel is kept
(`:402`), the Railway backend is kept, rollback is a DNS record with TTL 300.
That is a supportable end state and it is worth shipping on its own. With the
`FELLOWSHIP_ORIGIN` build-time fix (`WEBSITE.md` §1.2), the Stripe webhook fix
(§12) and the KV rate limiter (§11), it is a genuine improvement over today.

**Phase 6: no. There is no partial state.** `CUTOVER.md` 6.4 correctly refuses
dual-write (*"do not run two writable backends"*), so Phase 6 is one atomic
switch of the entire data layer, gated on a Worker that must serve all 26
collections and all 55 routes before it can be attempted at all. There is no
"move `events` first" option, because `guard.pb.js` is one middleware over one
generic API.

**Phase 7: yes.** The brain is repointed by one env var and a restart
(`brain/worker.py:43`), with Railway kept scaled-to-zero as the rollback
(`:512-513`) — subject to §3.3's caveat that `ANTICIPY_PB` and the Twilio
webhook must move together.

**The gap between 5 and 6 is where the risk concentrates**, and the plan has no
intermediate. One is available and should be added: **Phase 5.5 — the Worker in
read-only shadow.** `ARCHITECTURE.md` §12 Phase 1 already describes it (import
a snapshot, serve GET only, no client pointed at it, replay the real filter
strings from `extension/background.js:77-90` and `brain/worker.py:115-127`
against both, diff the `items` arrays). It is the single highest-value
verification in the whole plan and it is currently buried in a design document
rather than being a numbered cutover phase with a go/no-go.
